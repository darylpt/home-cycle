# Marketing and validation plan

## Positioning status

No market segment is validated. The approved 20-person simulation had 0 buyers; the same-seed scoped-offer retest also had 0. The panel is synthetic and its 15 habit / 5 need objections are hypotheses, not evidence about Philippine households.

**Working positioning hypothesis, not public copy:** For a household that loses track of when a recurring care task was last done, HomeCycle offers one shared date-based record with optional member attribution. Unlike a paper calendar, the record can show who logged a completion; unlike a chores app, HomeCycle is not currently claiming assignment rotation, reminders, or fairness. The single calendar supplied by the founder makes the job plausible, but competitors already offer recurrence, completion history, assignment, and household sharing.

No segment can be selected as “most likely to buy”: all simulated segments had a 0% buy rate. Test the one-routine use case with real couples and roommates before choosing a beachhead.

## Channels and budget

- **Start:** direct, no-cost recruitment for 10–15 household interviews, with both household members present where possible. This is a research channel hypothesis, not a proven acquisition channel.
- **Do not start:** paid social/search or creator spend. There is no validated audience, public product, price, or CAC ceiling; the founder specified no paid acquisition in the first 90 days.
- **Not selected:** Facebook groups, Messenger/Viber communities, creators, or app-store discovery. No real buyer evidence establishes which reaches this target or what moderation rules allow.
- **Cash budget:** ₱0 paid promotion, consistent with the founder's stated constraint. Founder hours and hosting are unknown, so total cost is not zeroed or estimated.
- **Maximum CAC:** not computable. No sale unit, contribution margin, retention, or real conversion exists.

## Field plan

No interview or pilot dates are set. The plan needs participant access and a safe test environment.

1. Recruit 10–15 households through direct contacts. Include couples and roommates. Ask each person for consent; invite both members where possible.
2. Start with five households. Ask about a recent care task that was forgotten or hard to coordinate, what happened, what record they use now, and who maintains it. Do not show HomeCycle or pitch features first.
3. Interview five more households. Include both members in at least three sessions. Find out whether the recurring problem is remembering, coordination, or fairness.
4. Only after privacy, account recovery, database isolation, and rollback are ready, invite 3–5 consenting households to test one real routine. Observe one member create the task, the second member join and log a completion, and both members check the history. Do not prompt the second member to log.
5. After seven days, check whether the shared history is still accurate. Ask participants who stopped using it what got in the way. Stop the test immediately if anyone can see another household's data.
6. Review the first five household tests. Continue only if at least four complete setup, at least three second members log without a reminder, at least three households have trustworthy history after seven days, and there are zero cross-household data exposures.

This is a research plan, not evidence that interviews or a pilot have happened. Recruitment, consent, and the safe test environment are still human prerequisites.

### Interview prompts

- Tell me about the last time a recurring care task was missed or hard to coordinate.
- What happened because it was missed or unclear?
- What do you use now to remember or share this information? Who updates it?
- When did another household member need the same information?
- What part of the current process would you keep, even if you changed tools?
- What would make a shared completion record inaccurate or untrustworthy?

## Copy hypotheses to test in interviews — not ready for ads

1. “When was this last done? Keep one shared record.”
2. “Stop guessing when a recurring care task was last completed.”
3. “Start with one household routine, not a full chore-system migration.”
4. “See the last completion date without searching old notes.”
5. “Optionally record who marked a task complete.”
6. “A shared history for mixed household care routines.”
7. “One date-based log; members decide how to divide the work.”
8. “Try the current MVP free when a private pilot is available.”
9. “Manual entry is required; test whether the shared history is worth that effort.”
10. “A completion log remembers what happened; it does not guarantee fairness.”

These are research prompts, not verified claims, customer quotes, testimonials, or launch ads.

## Measures and change thresholds

- Recruit 10–15 households; record how many include a second member in the session.
- Observe first-task setup and one real completion; stop if users do not understand what is recorded.
- Track whether the second member logs at least once in seven days without founder prompting. If fewer than 3 of the first 5 households do so, revisit the adoption loop before building marketing.
- Record the exact existing substitute and the consequence of a missed task; if no repeated high-consequence routine emerges, narrow or reconsider the market.
- No paid campaign, CAC target, or public launch threshold can be set from simulated panel answers.
