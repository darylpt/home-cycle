# Household validation plan

## Field plan

No interview or pilot dates are set. The plan needs participant access and a safe test environment.

1. Recruit 10–15 households through direct contacts. Include couples and roommates. Ask each person for consent; invite both members where possible.
2. Start with five households. Ask about a recent care task that was forgotten or hard to coordinate, what happened, what record they use now, and who maintains it. Do not show HomeCycle or pitch features first.
3. Interview five more households. Include both members in at least three sessions. Find out whether the recurring problem is remembering, coordination, or fairness.
4. Only after privacy, account recovery, database isolation, and rollback are ready, invite 3–5 consenting households to test one real routine. Observe one member create the task, the second member join and log a completion, and both members check the history. Do not prompt the second member to log.
5. After seven days, check whether the shared history is still accurate. Ask participants who stopped using it what got in the way. Stop the test immediately if anyone can see another household's data.
6. Review the first five household tests. Continue only if at least four complete setup, at least three second members log without a reminder, at least three households have trustworthy history after seven days, and there are zero cross-household data exposures.

This is a research plan, not evidence that interviews or a pilot have happened. Recruitment, consent, and the safe test environment are still human prerequisites.

### Interview prompts

- Tell me about the last time a recurring care task was missed or hard to coordinate.
- What happened because it was missed or unclear?
- What do you use now to remember or share this information? Who updates it?
- When did another household member need the same information?
- What part of the current process would you keep, even if you changed tools?
- What would make a shared completion record inaccurate or untrustworthy?

## Measures and change thresholds

- Recruit 10–15 households; record how many include a second member in the session.
- Observe first-task setup and one real completion; stop if users do not understand what is recorded.
- Track whether the second member logs at least once in seven days without founder prompting. If fewer than 3 of the first 5 households do so, revisit the adoption loop before building marketing.
- Record the exact existing substitute and the consequence of a missed task; if no repeated high-consequence routine emerges, narrow or reconsider the market.
